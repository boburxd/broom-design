## 6f. Honesty

What a person agrees to, pays and gives away is shown as it is. Every product type, unless the line says
otherwise; §2's dark-pattern ban is the short form.

1. Leaving is as easy as joining: cancelling, unsubscribing (in one click) and deleting the account are done
   by the person, through the same channel they signed up by.
2. Nothing paid, nothing that shares data and no mailing list is ticked in advance; an add-on is offered,
   never added.
3. The full price, with fees and delivery, shows before the last step, and where people pay there is a guest
   checkout (website, phone).
4. A refusal is a neutral word at readable contrast ("No, thanks"), never a line that shames and never a faint
   link; refusing cookies takes as little as accepting them.
5. Urgency and scarcity only when real: a timer never resets, and no invented "12 people are viewing" or "2
   left" (website, phone).
6. Content shows before a sign-in wall wherever it can; a phone's web page never blocks its content to push the
   app; nothing intercepts on entry or on exit, neither a mailing list nor a discount nor "get the app" (website,
   phone).
7. Permissions are never asked at first launch: the narrowest scope first (while in use, selected photos), "Not
   now" is not asked again in the same session, and the product still works after a refusal (phone).
8. Proof sits at the point of doubt, beside the price or the form, not only in a FAQ (website).
9. Demo content is labelled as a demo and leaves in one action.

## 6g. Chat and AI surfaces

1. A task with many required fields or parallel choices is a form or a table, never a chat.
2. A failed turn never asks its question again in the same words: the second attempt adds examples or options,
   the third offers a way out.
3. Structured results in a chat are cards with fields, and the next steps are a few reply chips.
4. An assistant never passes itself off as a person.
