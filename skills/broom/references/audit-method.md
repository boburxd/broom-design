## 6h. Audit and generation method

How an audit is judged and reported, and how a new direction is found.

1. A finding carries three proofs: which contract it breaks, the path by which the value reaches the surface,
   and the one fix that follows from that proof. A finding with two possible fixes, or one that guesses at
   intent, is dropped.
2. A product's system is read only from what drives its surface now: drafts, migrations and legacy code are not
   the system, a shared name or a similar token proves no link, and missing documentation is not a finding.
3. Hierarchy, emphasis, density and clarity are judged on the rendered screen, not in the source; a check that
   did not run is reported as not checked.
4. Every finding survives an attempt to disprove it. One cause is one finding, listing every place it shows and
   addressed to the token or the rule that produces it, with a confidence and a severity.
5. The cheapest fix comes first: delete, then take it from the platform, then reuse, then correct a value, and
   only then add. Proposing an addition where a deletion would do is itself a finding.
6. A report leads with about seven fixes, like ones grouped, each with its rule and its exact value. Blockers
   come first, the things that stop a person outright (no keyboard path, focus lost or invisible, text under
   its floor, a control with no name, zoom blocked, a dead end), then reach, then risk (§6a.40). Heading order
   is taste, not a blocker, unless it breaks moving by headings; someone else's small targets are judged by the
   24px circle (§6d.8).
7. After a round of fixes, look for what they broke: a contrast fix that washed out the brand, a new focus ring
   over a neighbour, the main element no longer leading.
8. Before a screen is called done, press every control once and write down "control → what happened"; what
   could not be run is marked as checked by reading.
9. Before a direction is chosen, name the three obvious defaults for this kind of interface and what replaces
   each. Then swap in a plain font and a stock layout: wherever nothing changes, the design had fallen back on
   the default.
10. A system handed over records every decision with its reason and its date.
11. Options come three to six, from conventional to new, at least one deliberately off the usual path. Each is
    described before it is built (its palette, its type pairing, its skeleton), differs on a real axis (layout,
    what leads, density, the model of interaction, the strategy for text) and is captioned by that axis;
    options that differ only in the accent are one. The set ends in one recommendation with its reason.
12. A direction is proven on one small surface before it spreads, and it is set in values (hex, fonts, radius,
    shadow, motion), never in adjectives.
13. Every multiple-choice question of a brief offers "Decide for me" and "Show me options" besides "Other". A
    question the supplied material already answers (a document, a repository, a brand) is shown as answered,
    not asked; questions run in order of how much they change the result, and one that changes nothing is
    dropped.
14. An ambiguous fix of hierarchy goes toward the stronger contrast.
15. Structure comes first, in greys and the system font, with real text and never lorem ipsum; the brand comes
    after.
