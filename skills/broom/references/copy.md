## 5. Copy rules

- Buttons: verb + object when the object is not obvious ("Export tokens"). Never "Submit", "OK",
  "Click here".
- Titles: nouns. Descriptions: one sentence, no period stacking.
- Errors: what happened + what to do ("Card declined. Try another card or contact your bank."). An
  error with a likely fix offers it as one action ("Use gmail.com"); a failure of the system is never worded
  as the person's fault. A field's
  error sits beside the field and instructs in the positive, with the limit ("Use at least 8 characters").
- Empty states: what the space is for + the first action.
- No exclamation marks, no first-person plural about the product ("we're excited"), no emoji outside a
  playful brief's copy and never one in a control. No "we" in errors or interface text, no needless "your",
  the person addressed as "you" and never as "the user", one form of address on a screen.
- No em dash: a comma, colon, full stop or parentheses. An en dash for ranges.
- Things are named for what the person controls ("notifications"), not for how the system is built
  ("webhooks"). Verbs fit the device: "tap" on touch, "click" with a mouse; help never explains a standard
  control.
- Menu items and column headers carry no spare words; column headers are nouns; a line of one sentence has
  no full stop.
- An ellipsis character ends a command that opens a further step before anything happens ("Rename…",
  "Export…") in a desktop web app; a phone's commands take none. A process status ends in one too
  ("Saving…").
- No "please", "sorry" or "thank you" in errors and routine messages; no "etc." or "i.e.". A pronoun or
  "this" always comes with its noun ("Couldn't move the photo", not "Couldn't move it").
- No idioms or slang; jargon and abbreviations are spelled out the first time. Gender-neutral wording, and
  gender asked only where it is needed, with "Prefer not to say".
- Each line of a heading, subheading and paragraph stack adds a fact; none restates the one above it.
- Bold in running text marks a rare phrase, never every term; no scare quotes; no list item written as a bold
  label followed by a restatement of it.
- The product is not given a mind: it never "understands", "knows" or "decides"; the text says what it does.
- One qualifier a claim at most, no filler, the verb first, instructions stated plainly rather than softened.
  This is an idea to apply, not a list of words to strike.
- Instructions never lean on colour, shape or position alone ("the green button on the right").
- Strings are whole sentences with placeholders and plural rules, never assembled from fragments.
- A list in running text holds about five items at most, in every product type.
- Numbering shown to people starts at one ("Step 1 of 4"), never at zero.
